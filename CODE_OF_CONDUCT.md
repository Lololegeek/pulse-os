# PulseOS Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

PulseOS contributors, maintainers, testers and users are expected to make participation in the project a respectful, harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of background, identity, experience level, disability, appearance, nationality, race, ethnicity, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, age, or other personal characteristics.

## Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that helps the project include:

- being respectful and technically constructive;
- assuming good intent while still reviewing claims critically;
- welcoming newcomers and explaining project-specific expectations;
- accepting correction and updating conclusions when evidence changes;
- focusing criticism on code, measurements and ideas rather than people;
- giving credit to upstream projects and other contributors.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, discrimination or personal attacks;
- sexualized language or unwanted sexual attention;
- doxxing or publishing another person's private information;
- deliberate intimidation, stalking or sustained disruption;
- insulting contributors because of hardware choices, skill level, language, or operating-system preferences;
- knowingly falsifying benchmarks or compatibility reports;
- encouraging others to bypass this policy.

## Scope

This code applies in repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews, project chat/discussions, release coordination, events, and when someone is representing PulseOS publicly.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, hide or remove contributions that violate this policy and may temporarily or permanently restrict participation for repeated or severe violations. Enforcement should be proportional, documented when appropriate, and focused on protecting the community.

For conduct concerns that can safely be public, open an issue and clearly mark it as a conduct concern. **Do not publish sensitive personal information in a public issue.** For private or safety-sensitive reports, use GitHub's private maintainer/security contact mechanisms when available.

Maintainers must handle reports in good faith and avoid unnecessary disclosure of reporter information.
